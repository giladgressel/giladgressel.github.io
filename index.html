Work featured at USENIX Security · ICLR · EMNLP · Black Hat USA · WIRED

I study how large language models and AI agents are misused for fraud, social engineering, and cybercrime, and how to monitor, attribute, and defend them. Much of my recent work reads a model's internal activations, rather than its output text, to see what it is doing and why.

My research has appeared at USENIX Security, ICLR, and EMNLP, received a USENIX Security Distinguished Paper runner-up award, and been covered by WIRED. I presented GAVEL at Black Hat USA 2026. I am advised by Krishnashree Achuthan at Amrita and Yisroel Mirsky at Ben-Gurion University. I hold an M.S. in Computer Science from Georgia Tech and a B.A. in Linguistics from UC Santa Cruz.

Alongside security, I work on responsible AI, with a focus on how LLMs behave across languages and cultures, and I support medical AI research with Maitri Labs.

I am open to research collaborations, speaking invitations, and consulting on AI security.

Selected Research

USENIX Security 2026 Distinguished Paper Runner-up WIRED

Love, Lies, and Language Models: Investigating AI's Role in Romance-Baiting Scams

Scam compounds already use LLMs to groom victims. In a seven-day blinded study, participants trusted an LLM agent more than a human operator, and 46% followed its final request, against 18% for the human. Current content filters caught none of the scam conversations.

Paper Project Code & data WIRED
ICLR 2026 Black Hat USA 2026

GAVEL: Towards Rule-Based Safety through Activation Monitoring

Safety rules written over a model's internal activations instead of its output text. Lightweight probes detect interpretable concepts, and logical rules combine them, so policies can be written, updated, and shared without retraining the model.

Paper Project Code Black Hat talk
EMNLP 2026

PRISM: Recovering Instruction Sets from Language Model Activations

PRISM reads the instructions steering a model directly from its activations, without seeing the prompt. Trained only on ordinary chat data, it caught 94.1% of the prompt injections the model acted on across three benchmarks.
